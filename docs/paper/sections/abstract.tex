\begin{abstract}
Messages between language-model agents cost time and energy, especially on edge hardware. Three levers reduce that cost: say less by filtering the text, reuse computation by caching, or send a latent message instead of text. Latent communication has been studied only between transformers, yet sensing agents are often not transformers. Which lever suits them? A heartbeat classifier passes each event to a frozen Gemma~4 E4B model as text or through a learned linear adapter that emits four virtual tokens per event. The text arms include the strongest alternative, a message listing only abnormal events. The task is to report the most urgent triage tier among 1 to 50 heartbeats. It is a communication-fidelity benchmark: the correct answer is a fixed rule over the sender's outputs, so the receiver stands in for a general-purpose agent and the measure is what each channel costs and preserves, not what the language model adds. The evaluation follows a pre-specified analysis plan with three seeds and held-out patients. Against compact text, the adapter cut prompt-processing time by 24\%, 50\% and 78\% at 10, 20 and 50 events and was more accurate than calibrated text at 10 and 20 events (balanced accuracy 0.83 against 0.73). Filtered text was 0.11 to 0.17 more accurate and as fast for one request, but its length varies with content: under batched serving the adapter delivered 10 to 30\% more decisions per second at 12 to 28\% less energy. Heart rate, interval and abnormal-run information were recoverable from the virtual tokens. The pattern largely held on a second sender and on an external database (INCART), where the adapter again beat calibrated text and filtered text again beat the adapter. A sender-side filter is the better interface when the receiver's question is known in advance; a latent channel earns its place when many events must be carried at a predictable cost.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Latent channel from a non-transformer sender compared with filtered text
\item Adapter carries up to 50 events at four tokens each into a frozen model
\item Filtered text is more accurate; the adapter wins under batched serving
\item Per-slot, per-field recoverability of the virtual tokens is measured
\item A selection rule, the analysis plan and its change log are released
\end{highlights}
