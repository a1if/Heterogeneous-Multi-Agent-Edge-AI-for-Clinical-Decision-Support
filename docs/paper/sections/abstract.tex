\begin{abstract}
The cost of messages between language-model agents is now a design constraint, especially on edge hardware. Three levers exist: say less by filtering the text, reuse computation by caching, or send a latent message instead of text. Latent communication has been studied only between transformer models, yet the agents that sense the world are often convolutional or recurrent networks. We ask which lever suits such a sender. A heartbeat classifier passes each event to a frozen Gemma~4 E4B model either as text or through a learned linear adapter that emits four virtual tokens per event. The text arms include the strongest engineering alternative, a message listing only the abnormal events. On one task, reporting the most urgent triage tier among 1 to 50 consecutive heartbeats, we compare cost, accuracy and recoverability under a pre-registered protocol with three training seeds, held-out patients and patient-level confidence intervals. Against compact text, the adapter cut prompt-processing time by 24\%, 50\% and 78\% and time to decision by 4\%, 14\% and 36\% at 10, 20 and 50 events, and was more accurate than calibrated text at 10 and 20 events (balanced accuracy 0.83 against 0.73). Filtered text was 0.11 to 0.17 more accurate and equally fast for one request, but its length varies with content, so under batched serving the adapter delivered 10 to 30\% more decisions per second at 12 to 28\% less energy. Heart rate, interval and abnormal-run information were recoverable from the virtual tokens, and an ablation showed that passing them drove the adapter's accuracy gain and its lower false-alarm rate. Four tokens per event was the smallest reliable setting: with fewer, the information stayed recoverable but its use became unreliable. The pattern held on a second sender architecture and, in a pre-registered test on an external database (INCART), the adapter was again more accurate than calibrated text at 10 and 20 events while filtered text was again more accurate than the adapter. A sender-side filter is the better interface when the receiver's question is known in advance; a latent channel earns its place when many events must be carried at a predictable cost.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Latent channel from a non-transformer sender compared with filtered text
\item Adapter carries up to 50 events at four tokens each into a frozen model
\item Filtered text is more accurate; the adapter wins under batched serving
\item Per-slot, per-field recoverability of the virtual tokens is measured
\item A selection rule and the pre-registration log are released
\end{highlights}
